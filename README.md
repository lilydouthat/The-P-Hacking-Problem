# P-Hacking: How Data Can Lie

Overview

This project explores p-hacking, a statistical practice in which researchers manipulate analyses, selectively report results, or repeatedly test hypotheses until statistically significant findings are obtained. The project examines how these practices can contribute to misleading conclusions in research and media reporting.

Using examples from scientific studies and popular headlines, this presentation investigates why research findings often appear contradictory and highlights the importance of transparency, reproducibility, and ethical statistical practices.

⸻

Research Questions

* What is p-hacking?
* Why does p-hacking occur?
* How does p-hacking affect scientific credibility?
* Why do research headlines frequently contradict one another?
* What can researchers do to improve reproducibility and trust in scientific findings?

⸻

Key Findings

* Statistical significance can sometimes be achieved through repeated testing rather than meaningful discoveries.
* Selective reporting increases the likelihood of false-positive findings.
* Publication bias encourages the publication of significant results while discouraging null findings.
* P-hacking contributes to confusion among researchers, policymakers, and the public.
* Research transparency, pre-registration, and replication studies help improve scientific reliability.
